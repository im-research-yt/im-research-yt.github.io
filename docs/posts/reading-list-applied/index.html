QCA in marketing and management: five papers to read first (applications)

Papers that use QCA in marketing and management, and practical guides on how to run and report an analysis.
QCA
marketing
notes
Author

Yuki Toyoda

Published

October 6, 2026

The basics list introduced the readings that explain how QCA thinks. The next question is probably “How is it actually used in research?” Here are papers from marketing and management that are worth reading as models for your own work, grouped by purpose.

As in the basics list, the summaries stay within what the publishers’ abstracts say.

1. Applications in marketing and services

  • Ordanini, A., Parasuraman, A., & Rubera, G. (2014). When the recipe is more important than the ingredients: A qualitative comparative analysis (QCA) of service innovation configurations. Journal of Service Research, 17(2), 134-149. https://doi.org/10.1177/1094670513513337 Studies luxury hotel services and uses QCA to examine which combinations of attributes lead to the adoption of a new service. An application that looks not only at individual attributes but at how they are combined.
  • Frambach, R. T., Fiss, P. C., & Ingenbleek, P. T. M. (2016). How important is customer orientation for firm performance? A fuzzy set analysis of orientations, strategies, and environments. Journal of Business Research, 69(4), 1428-1436. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbusres.2015.10.120 Combines customer orientation with other strategic orientations, strategy types, and market environments, and uses fsQCA to find the configurations that go with high firm performance. An application that considers the role of customer orientation through its combinations with other conditions.

2. How to run and report an analysis (practical guides)

  • Greckhamer, T., Furnari, S., Fiss, P. C., & Aguilera, R. V. (2018). Studying configurations with qualitative comparative analysis: Best practices in strategy and organization research. Strategic Organization, 16(4), 482-495. https://doi.org/10.1177/1476127018786487 Sets out practical guidelines for QCA in strategy and organization research. It covers the whole course of a study, from model building and case selection to calibration, analysis, reporting, and interpretation.
  • Pappas, I. O., & Woodside, A. G. (2021). Fuzzy-set qualitative comparative analysis (fsQCA): Guidelines for research practice in information systems and marketing. International Journal of Information Management, 58, Article 102310. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijinfomgt.2021.102310 A practical guide to using fsQCA in information systems and marketing research. It explains the procedure with data from earlier studies and also touches on checking predictive validity and combining fsQCA with methods such as SEM.
  • Park, Y., Fiss, P. C., & El Sawy, O. A. (2020). Theorizing the multiplicity of digital phenomena: The ecology of configurations, causal recipes, and guidelines for applying QCA. MIS Quarterly, 44(4), 1493-1520. https://doi.org/10.25300/MISQ/2020/13879 For digital phenomena that involve several theoretical perspectives and combinations of conditions, it offers a framework that ties QCA to theory building. Through examples, it explains how to apply QCA to deductive theory testing and to inductive theory building.

On how the placement of the threshold changes the results, see also Toyoda (2026), Quality & Quantity, https://doi.org/10.1007/s11135-026-03092-3.
